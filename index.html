Overview

Following an age cue is not the same as knowing an age group

An age prompt can change how a vision-language model (VLM) rates an image. Does the change predict how people of different ages rate that same image? We evaluate 17 VLMs on 900 OASIS images and estimate image-specific age differences from ratings by 822 participants. Without an age cue, models closely track average human valence (median Pearson r = 0.891). Under an older-adult prompt, 16 of 17 models assign lower valence on average than under a young-adult prompt. Yet after adjusting for image pleasantness, correlations between model and human age effects range from −0.030 to 0.093, against an estimated ceiling of 0.595.

The discrepancy is clearest on 54 sensitive images rated as pleasant. Across models, the older prompt reduces valence by 0.845 points on average, while older participants rate these images 0.443 points higher than younger participants. Providing human ratings from other images improves agreement on the direction of age differences, although image-specific fidelity remains modest on 600 held-out images (rpart = 0.105). These results show why evaluations of demographic personalization need human comparisons at the level of individual stimuli.

How the human and model age effects are estimated §3.1 – §3.3

Only 41 raters are 60+, so per-image older means are unreliable. A partially pooled model gives each image its own age slope \(s_i\):

\[ y_{pi}^{(d)} = \alpha_{\ell(i)}^{(d)} + \beta_{\ell(i)}^{(d)} z_p^{(d)} + u_p^{(d)} + v_i^{(d)} + s_i^{(d)} z_p^{(d)} + \varepsilon_{pi}^{(d)}, \qquad \Delta H_i^{(d)} = \mathbb{E}\big[s_i^{(d)} \mid \mathcal{D}_H\big] \]

The model age effect is the least-squares trend across the young, middle and older personas (reference ages 23.5, 44.5, 67.0), rescaled to the human units:

\[ \Delta M_{im}^{(d)} = \sigma_a^{(d)} \frac{\sum_g (a_g-\bar a_G)\, M_{img}^{(d)}}{\sum_g (a_g-\bar a_G)^2}, \qquad g \in \{Y, M, O\} \]

Pleasantness alone correlates with \(\Delta H\) at 0.897, so the headline statistic \(r_{\text{part}}\) partials a quadratic in human valence out of both sides. Its attainable ceiling, set by uncertainty in the human effects, is 0.595 (split-half check: 0.62). Temperature 0, one completion, no system prompt; the persona prompts differ only in the age phrase.

Robustness checks §4.1 and appendix
  • Human reference. With raters and images resampled and the human model refit, no valence interval lies above zero. Median rpart stays ≤ 0.05 under ten estimators of the human age effect, including a 50+ contrast with 78 raters.
  • Power. Planting known fidelity into the OASIS design gives 92% power at a true fidelity of 0.20.
  • Prompts. The valence results hold across seven further prompt formats, and without the description field (6 of 6 models).
  • Second dataset. On 450 NAPS images, six models reach median valence r = 0.89 (0.90 on OASIS); with NAPS raters averaging 23.9 years, a young-adult cue never measurably lowers valence error.
  • Shift ≠ fidelity. Across models, the size of the uniform shift and rpart correlate at r = −0.038 (p = 0.88).

A stereotype, not a population. A no-age “retired” persona reproduces the older-persona shift (median per-image r = 0.75) and its memory language, whereas a “cautious” persona lowers ratings across all images (r = 0.46). Across models, the size of the valence shift tracks how often the older persona reminisces (ρ = −0.76), not baseline competence (ρ = −0.07).

Interventions

Reasoning does not help. Human evidence partly does.

Six interventions on GPT-5.6-Luna-Pro, selected on 300 development images, then the chosen arm run unchanged on 600 held-out images. Evidence comes only from other OASIS lists and themes, never the target image.

Fidelity after removing pleasantness

95% bootstrap CI · dashed line: human ceiling

The honest baseline. A pleasantness-only rule with no model still beats every arm on direction agreement and error, because pleasantness explains 81% of the human age effect. That is why rpart, which removes pleasantness, is the target: held out, S4 reaches 0.105 [0.014, 0.196], above direct prompting by +0.130 (p = .041), and fixes 400 image directions while breaking 35.

Takeaways

What this means for personalized assistants

Evaluate

Compare contrasts, not averages

High agreement with a pooled norm can coexist with a reversed group difference. Score model contrasts against human contrasts on the same stimuli.

Control

Partial out the obvious

Pleasantness alone predicts the human age effect at r = 0.897. Report fidelity after removing it, against a ceiling set by the human data.

Caution

Age cues can hurt the target group

Predicting raters aged 60+, median RMSE rises from 0.756 to 0.789 with the older persona, and from 0.754 to 1.214 on pleasant sensitive images.

Limitations §5
  • Raters are aged 18–74, with 41 aged 60+ (25 contributing valence). The design is cross-sectional and the sample is an online U.S. one, so the human contrasts describe age differences in that population.
  • OASIS provides the reference norms; arousal age effects are supported for only 59 of 900 images, so arousal fidelity is not interpreted.
  • Each request uses one completion at temperature 0. The interventions use one backbone (GPT-5.6-Luna-Pro), which limits conclusions about others.

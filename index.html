Abstract

An age prompt can change how a vision-language model (VLM) rates an image. Does the change predict how people of different ages rate that same image? We evaluate 17 VLMs on 900 OASIS images and estimate image-specific age differences from ratings by 822 participants. Without an age cue, models closely track average human valence (median Pearson r = 0.891). Under an older-adult prompt, 16 of 17 models assign lower valence on average than under a young-adult prompt. Yet after adjusting for image pleasantness, correlations between model and human age effects range from −0.030 to 0.093, against an estimated ceiling of 0.595. The discrepancy is clearest on 54 sensitive images rated as pleasant. Across models, the older prompt reduces valence by 0.845 points on average, while older participants rate these images 0.443 points higher than younger participants. Providing human ratings from other images improves agreement on the direction of age differences, although image-specific fidelity remains modest on 600 held-out images (rpart = 0.105). These results show why evaluations of demographic personalization need human comparisons at the level of individual stimuli.

Method

We use all 900 OASIS images and the participant-level archive of 184,939 ratings from 822 people aged 18–74. Each of 17 instruction-tuned VLMs from seven developers rates every image under four instructions that specify no age, young adulthood, middle adulthood, or older adulthood, returning valence, arousal, an emotion label and a short description (61,200 requests, 99.6% valid). We treat model outputs as instructed judgments, not evidence of felt emotion, and separate three properties:

Competence
Agreement of the no-age rating with the pooled human mean \(\bar H_i\): Pearson r, CCC, MAE and calibration slope.
Responsiveness
The paired older-minus-young contrast \(\Delta_{O-Y}\) on the same images, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across models.
Fidelity
The partial correlation \(r_{\text{part}}\) between the model age effect \(\Delta M_i\) and the human age effect \(\Delta H_i\), controlling for pleasantness.
Human and model age effects

Only 41 participants are aged 60 or older, so older-adult means are not reliable for individual images. We estimate image-specific age effects with a partially pooled model fitted by variational EM:

\[ y_{pi}^{(d)} = \alpha_{\ell(i)}^{(d)} + \beta_{\ell(i)}^{(d)} z_p^{(d)} + u_p^{(d)} + v_i^{(d)} + s_i^{(d)} z_p^{(d)} + \varepsilon_{pi}^{(d)}, \qquad \Delta H_i^{(d)} = \mathbb{E}\big[s_i^{(d)} \mid \mathcal{D}_H\big] \]

The model age effect is the least-squares trend across the young, middle and older personas (reference ages 23.5, 44.5 and 67.0), rescaled to the human units:

\[ \Delta M_{im}^{(d)} = \sigma_a^{(d)} \frac{\sum_g (a_g-\bar a_G)\, M_{img}^{(d)}}{\sum_g (a_g-\bar a_G)^2}, \qquad g \in \{Y, M, O\} \]

Because pooled pleasantness alone correlates with \(\Delta H\) at r = 0.897, \(r_{\text{part}}\) partials a quadratic in human valence out of both sides. Its attainable ceiling, given uncertainty in the human effects, is 0.595 (split-half estimate 0.62). All requests use temperature 0, one completion, and no system message; the persona prompts differ only in the age phrase.

Results

Without an age cue, agreement with pooled human valence is high for every developer (r = 0.837–0.923, median 0.891), and all 17 models over-disperse the human range (calibration slopes 1.04–1.39). Under the age instruction, 16 of 17 models rate the same images lower under the older persona than under the young persona, 13 after correction. Yet after list centering and partialling out pleasantness, the image-level correlation with the human age effect lies in [−0.030, 0.093], against an attainable ceiling of 0.595. The best model reaches 15.6% of that ceiling.

Models agree with each other more than with humans

The mean pairwise correlation of model age effects over the 136 model pairs is 0.43 (range 0.16–0.71), with similar estimates within and across developers (0.424 vs. 0.429, permutation p = 0.54). One principal component carries 47% of the variance with all 17 models loading positively, and the consensus has split-half reliability 0.93. Yet the consensus correlates with the human age effect at only 0.050 [−0.02, 0.13]. Its first component tracks human arousal (r = −0.47) and the sensitive flag (r = −0.33), not human valence (r = 0.01).

Robustness checks
  • With raters and images resampled and the human model refitted, no valence interval lies above zero (largest upper bound 0.187).
  • Median rpart stays at or below 0.05 under ten estimators of the human age effect, including a 50+ contrast with 78 raters.
  • Planting known fidelity into the OASIS design gives 92% power at a true fidelity of 0.20.
  • The valence results hold across seven further prompt formats and without a description (6 of 6 models).
  • On 450 NAPS images, six models reach a median valence correlation of 0.89 (0.90 on OASIS). A young-adult cue does not measurably reduce valence RMSE against the NAPS norms, whose raters averaged 23.9 years.
  • Across models, the size of the uniform shift and rpart correlate at r = −0.038 (p = 0.88).

Sensitive Content

The shared response is associated more strongly with sensitive content than with pleasantness: the older-minus-young valence shift averages −0.390 on flagged images and −0.035 on unflagged ones, an ordering that holds for all 17 models. Crossing the flag with pooled human valence shows where models and humans disagree. On flagged unpleasant images (violence, injury, death), all models share the sign of the human contrast. On flagged images that humans rate as pleasant, mostly nudity and erotic content, every model falls below the human interval.

746 pairs receive an aversive label under the older persona only, against 239 under the young persona only; the gain is significant for 13 of 17 models. Label entropy is unchanged (2.49 vs. 2.47 bits).

Within each model, vulnerability language accompanies lower older-persona ratings, while memory language accompanies higher ratings. Since ratings and descriptions come from the same generation, these associations do not establish causality. A no-age “retired” persona reproduces the older-persona shift (median per-image r = 0.75) and its memory language, whereas a “cautious” persona lowers ratings across all images (r = 0.46). The response to the retirement cue supports a stereotype-based interpretation of the shared pattern.

Effects of Reasoning and Human Evidence

We evaluate six interventions with GPT-5.6-Luna-Pro on a stratified subset of 300 images. S1 adds an instruction not to rely on age stereotypes; S2 chains a perceiver, a persona rater, a stereotype critic and a reviser, with no human data. S3–S5 and VERA-Cal use retrieved human age differences for similar images from other OASIS lists and themes, so the model never sees ratings for the target image. The selected arm, S4, is then run unchanged on the 600 remaining images.

Reasoning alone does not improve alignment: S1 and S2 reach mean rpart = −0.01, compared with 0.06 for the original VLMs, and direction agreement falls from 51% to 42%. Human-grounded agents raise direction agreement to 83% (p < .001). Direction agreement is partly driven by pleasantness, which explains 81% of the human age effect, and a pleasantness-only rule with no model still exceeds every arm on direction agreement and error. On the 600 held-out images, S4 reaches rpart = 0.105 [0.014, 0.196], above direct prompting by +0.130 [0.006, 0.254] (p = .041).

Discussion

Strong agreement with average human ratings does not necessarily translate into capturing age-related differences in image perception. The gap is clearest for pleasant images flagged as sensitive in OASIS, where models assign lower valence under the older persona although older participants rate these images more positively. Age prompting can also reduce predictive accuracy for the intended group: across 900 images, median RMSE for participants aged 60 and above increases from 0.756 to 0.789, driven largely by the 54 pleasant sensitive images, where it rises from 0.754 to 1.214. Evaluations of age-conditioned predictions should compare model and human contrasts on matched images and report agreement after accounting for pleasantness.

Limitations. Raters are aged up to 74, including 41 aged 60 and above (25 contributing valence ratings). The design is cross-sectional and the sample is an online U.S. one. OASIS provides the reference norms, and arousal age effects are supported for only 59 of 900 images. Each request uses a single completion at temperature 0, and the interventions use one backbone.
